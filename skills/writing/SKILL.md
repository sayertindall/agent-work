# Writing

Establish the reader, purpose, source facts, and requested form. Preserve the author's intended meaning and voice. Organize around what the reader needs to understand or do; remove filler and duplicated claims.

Use concrete verbs and familiar terms. Keep necessary technical distinctions and evidence. A short sentence is not better if it conceals a condition or omits the reasoning the reader needs. Use tables or lists when the information is truly parallel.

Check names, claims, and commitments against supplied evidence. Do not invent facts to make prose persuasive. Flag unresolved facts. Drafting a message does not authorize sending it; drafting a report does not authorize publication. Return the requested text without unnecessary editorial commentary.
